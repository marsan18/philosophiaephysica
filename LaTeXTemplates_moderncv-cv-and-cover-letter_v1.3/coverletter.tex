I write regarding the exciting opportunity to work as a temporary instructor of Physics I and II at UNC-Greensboro, as advertised via announcement. I am currently a doctoral candidate at UNC-Chapel Hill; I have earned my Master's of Science in Physics and have completed all requirements for my doctorate except my dissertation, which I anticipate defending in Spring 2027. As a Royster Doctoral Fellow with non-service funding, I am available to fill the listed position this spring. \par
My extensive experience as a physics instructor includes a long history of working with students enrolled in introductory physics courses; I have acted as a teacher's assistant at both Wake Forest and UNC-Chapel Hill for these courses. My time teaching was formative, orienting me towards the efficacy of an experiential learning process where students develop an enhanced understanding of curricula via interactive modules. Additionally, I have eight years as a private physics tutor, which has provided me with first-hand insight into the diverse learning styles of students from all walks of life. \par

I believe that it is crucial that students see the connection between thier curricula and how that knowledge can be applied to what is most important in life. As an educator, I benefit from years of philosophical study, which helps give me a deeply grounded, functionalist approach to physics focused on how theory can be applied dynamically to understand and positively shape the world around us. My educational experiences are also buttressed by years of experimental biophysics research, which gives a broad applied knowledge base, empowering me to teach with expertise and nuance. Recently, I mentored a team of undergraduates, guiding them to build a 3D-printed fluorescent microscope capable of advanced particle tracking from inexpensive, commerically available parts. The team went on to win awards for their work at consecutive annual UNC MakerFest competitions; we are now coorperating with low-income high schools in Eastern NC to conduct these builds locally, giving them access to microscopy tools which would normally cost tens of thousands of dollars. These experiances have prepared me to help students understand that studying physics is about more than lab experiments and a curricular requirement, instead offering an empowering understanding of daily life.\par

Thank you for your time, and I look forward to talking with you about this exciting opportunity in the coming weeks.\par
